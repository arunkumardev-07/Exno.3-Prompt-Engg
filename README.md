### Algorithm:  1. Direct Instruction Prompts
Scenario-based report development in the context of artificial intelligence, particularly when designing conversational agents or chatbots, involves structuring the AI’s interaction logic around real-life situations. This approach is not only crucial for effective prompt engineering but also enhances the usability, contextual relevance, and adaptability of the AI system. By embedding the use of diverse prompting techniques such as Straightforward Prompting, Tabular Format Prompting, and Preceding Question Prompting, developers can guide AI behavior in a structured yet flexible manner, aligning system responses more closely with user expectations.

In a scenario-based report, one imagines a real-world interaction where a user might contact an AI assistant for help. For instance, consider a customer service chatbot in an e-commerce setting. A user may reach out to inquire about the status of their order, ask how to fix a product issue, or learn more about the company’s return policy. The goal of the scenario is to create a realistic environment in which different prompting methods can be tested and evaluated for their effectiveness in eliciting relevant, accurate, and empathetic responses from the AI.

Straightforward prompting is often the most direct approach to guide the AI. It involves issuing a simple, clear instruction without much context. In the chatbot scenario, this might be a prompt like, “Track order #4532,” or “Explain how to reset the router.” While this method is efficient, it may not always account for the nuanced needs of the user, especially in complex queries. However, it serves well for straightforward tasks, especially when rapid responses are needed or when training the model to handle high-volume FAQs.

Tabular format prompting introduces a more structured way of presenting information to the AI. This is particularly useful in customer service contexts where multiple types of data or categories are involved. For example, presenting a prompt such as, “Based on the table of customer complaints and resolutions, suggest how to respond to a customer who received a broken item,” enables the AI to understand the logical mappings between problems and solutions. The use of tables can help the model comprehend relationships and hierarchies, enhancing its decision-making capabilities and enabling the delivery of more accurate and context-sensitive responses.

Preceding question prompting, also known as sequential or contextual prompting, is an advanced technique in which each new prompt builds upon the response or question that came before it. This mirrors human conversation and is essential in sustaining context within a dialogue. For example, a user may first ask, “Can I return a damaged item?” followed by, “What if I lost the receipt?” In such cases, the AI must remember and connect the initial question to the follow-up to give a coherent and complete answer. This technique is especially valuable in long conversations, where maintaining the continuity and context of the interaction is critical.
